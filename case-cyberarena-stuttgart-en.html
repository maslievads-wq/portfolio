€0.78 per message and €0.56 per follower: a cyber arena demand test in a Tier-1 country

€0.78 per message €0.56 per follower Meta Ads Germany · Tier-1

Demand test for a cyber arena in Stuttgart (Germany) on Meta Ads before opening: messages from €0.78 and followers at €0.56 — validated demand before the venue launch.

About the project

  • Project: cyber arena / gaming club;
  • Location: Germany, Stuttgart;
  • Channel: Meta Ads;
  • Stage: before opening — before buying equipment and launching the venue;
  • Goal: validate demand for the cyber-arena format for an investor;
  • Result: messages from €0.78, follower at €0.56.

Objective

Before launching a cyber arena in Germany, we needed to understand whether there is real demand for the gaming-club format in Stuttgart.

The project required serious investment: premises, renovation, equipment, legal setup, a team and an operating model. So before investing it was important to test not just interest in games, but the audience's readiness to engage with the project.

The key question for the investor: would the German audience be interested in a cyber arena even before construction and full launch? We needed to check:

  • whether people would send messages;
  • whether they'd ask where the club is located;
  • whether they'd follow the page;
  • whether they'd like and engage with content;
  • whether a local community could be gathered in Stuttgart in advance;
  • whether the cyber-arena format has marketing potential in Germany.

Approach

Testing demand via an abstract survey ('would you like a gaming club in Stuttgart?') is pointless — such answers poorly reflect real behavior. Someone may say 'yes' but never follow or message.

So we packaged the project as if the cyber arena already existed or was about to open in Stuttgart. The audience saw not an idea but a concrete place — Cyber Arena Stuttgart: a gaming space with powerful PCs, team games, tournaments and an esports-club atmosphere. This let us test demand in conditions close to a real launch.

What we tested

Several scenarios through which the audience could get interested in the cyber arena:

  • Cyber arena for gamers — direct demand for the format: powerful PCs, fast internet, gaming zones, team play without buying an expensive computer;
  • Playing with friends — the arena as a place for shared leisure ('gather your friends and come play'), competing with cinema, bars, bowling, VR;
  • Tournaments and gaming community — interest in local tournaments and a competitive format;
  • Birthdays and events — a venue for birthdays, teen parties and corporate events;
  • Retargeting — working with the audience that already watched videos, visited the profile, liked posts and opened the ads.

What was done

We prepared the project's ad packaging for the German market:

  • positioning of the cyber arena for the German market;
  • offers for different visit scenarios;
  • ad copy in German;
  • visuals in the format of an already-existing club;
  • campaigns for messages, engagement and follows;
  • retargeting of the interested audience;
  • segment tests: gamers, students, groups of friends, parents, events audience.

The main goal wasn't to sell a visit (the club wasn't open yet), but to collect real demand signals: someone messaged, asked the address, followed, liked, started following the project.

Results

The test showed a strong audience response. The best campaigns delivered messages at €0.78 — a very strong figure for Germany, especially for a local offline project in a Tier-1 country rather than a cheap mass info-product.

More important than volume was the organic interest: people messaged in DMs, asked the address and opening details, liked posts, followed the page and reacted to the ads as if to a real place in the city. The follower cost was especially strong — €0.56 per follower, very low for the German market and a local offline project. In effect, the audience could be gathered in advance, before opening, forming the first community.

What the test showed the investor

  • Demand exists — the Stuttgart audience messaged, followed, liked and asked questions;
  • The project can be promoted before opening — interest and first contacts gathered without a physical venue;
  • There's local-community potential — follows, messages and engagement confirmed it;
  • The 'already-existing place' framing worked — people asked concrete questions: where it is, when it opens, how to get in;
  • Marketing risk turned out low — cheap messages, cheap follows, high interest.

Why the project wasn't launched

Despite strong ad metrics, the project didn't reach a physical launch — but not because of a lack of demand or a weak marketing model. It stopped due to legal and organizational nuances of launching an offline business in Germany: premises requirements, permits, regulation, operational constraints and legal specifics.

From an advertising and demand standpoint the test was a success. The constraints appeared at the legal and operational implementation stage.
