From customer development to 1,000+ active B2B clients: launching a buy-now-pay-later service for e-commerce

1,000+ B2B clients Customer Development Partner Ecosystem Unlimited LTV

Built and launched a B2B installment-payment (BNPL) service for e-commerce: from customer development and pilot integrations to a partner ecosystem with web studios — onboarded 1,000+ active B2B clients in the first year with near-unlimited LTV.

The task

The goal was to launch a B2B installment-payment service that would increase product accessibility for end buyers. The core challenge: the technical ability to offer installments wasn't a sufficient argument for a business on its own — it had to be shown how the product affects sales, average order value and customer experience.

  • Identify the business's real needs
  • Shape a product module for the target audience
  • Run the first integrations
  • Validate companies' readiness to adopt the service
  • Build a clear B2B sales funnel
  • Find scalable acquisition channels
  • Build a partner model for distribution

Audience research

Formed a focus group of online store owners who already sold online but didn't offer installment payments to customers, and ran customer development interviews.

  • Reasons installments hadn't been adopted yet
  • Technical constraints and integration requirements
  • Business owners' concerns
  • Expectations of the impact on sales
  • Current site structure and checkout process
  • Criteria for choosing a payment service

After the first interviews, pilot integrations began — turning feedback from hypotheses into signal from real product usage.

Shaping the product

Based on customer development and the first rollouts, shaped a product module with key requirements:

  • Simple onboarding
  • Minimal load on the client's team
  • Clear user journey
  • Integration into the existing e-commerce process
  • Ability to scale across different business types

The product evolved iteratively: after every integration, feedback was gathered, issues were analyzed, and requirements refined.

Expanding the target audience

It became clear that part of the potential client base couldn't adopt the service for technical reasons: no website, an outdated site, a required redesign, or no full online store at all. Instead of excluding those clients, built a partner model.

Partner ecosystem

For companies without ready e-commerce infrastructure, onboarded partners who built online stores, handled redesigns, upgraded checkout, and integrated the installment service. This gave the client a full solution:

  • A website or redesign
  • E-commerce setup
  • Installment-service integration
  • Sales launch

Working with digital studios

A separate track became a B2B model through web and digital studios, which integrated the installment service into their own clients' projects — enabling scale through partners with an existing client flow instead of acquiring every business directly.

Audience segments

Online stores

Companies with a working site that needed to add installment payments.

Offline businesses

Companies that wanted to use installments as an extra sales tool.

Businesses without a ready site

Companies that first needed e-commerce infrastructure built or modernized.

Digital and web studios

Partners who integrated the service into their clients' projects.

The B2B funnel

Built a dedicated sales logic for account managers:

  • Business segmentation
  • Assessing current infrastructure
  • Defining the onboarding scenario
  • Demonstrating value
  • Technical assessment
  • Integration
  • Launch

Messaging differed by segment: for online stores — conversion growth, higher average order value and a lower purchase barrier; for businesses without a site — launching e-commerce and full integration; for studios — an expanded product offering and the partner model.

Go-to-market strategy

The go-to-market strategy ran on several tracks at once: direct B2B sales, account managers, pilot integrations, partnerships with web studios and developers, and full-service projects for companies without a site — building not one acquisition channel but a whole distribution ecosystem.

Why the model proved durable

The product solved several business problems at once: an extra payment method, a lower purchase barrier, a higher average order value, integration into existing infrastructure, and no need for repeated re-implementation. Once adopted, the service became part of the client's e-commerce process — which is why client relationships were long-term.
